How it works

Age is counted in completed years, then months, then days

It sounds simple, but calendars make it fiddly: months are 28 to 31 days long, and every fourth year (usually) adds a 29 February. This calculator does the borrowing properly, so the answer is the age you would give on a form — not an approximation from dividing days by 365.

  1. 1

    Count the years

    Subtract the birth year from the current year, then take one year off if this year’s birthday has not happened yet.

  2. 2

    Count the months

    Count the completed months since the last birthday, borrowing a month when the current day of the month is earlier than the birth day.

  3. 3

    Count the days

    The days left over, using the actual length of the previous month — so a result is always a real calendar age, never a rounded one.

Worked example

Someone born on 1 January 2000, calculated on 3 November 2026:

2026 − 2000 = 26 years  →  January to November is 10 completed months  →  1 November to 3 November is 2 days.

Age: 26 years, 10 months and 2 days. The same person has lived 9,803 days, including 7 leap days (29 February in 2000, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024).

Leap years, and being born on 29 February

A year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4, except century years, which must be divisible by 400 — so 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 and 2100 are not. If you were born on 29 February, your birthday only exists every four years. For both the age calculation and the countdown on this page, 28 February is used as the birthday in non-leap years, which is the common convention; some people prefer to celebrate on 1 March instead. Either way, your age in years still increases once a year.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How is age calculated?

Age is the time between your date of birth and the date you are calculating for, counted in completed years, then completed months, then the remaining days — using real calendar month lengths and leap years, not an average of 365 days per year.

Is my date of birth sent or stored anywhere?

No. Every calculation on this page runs in your own browser. Your date of birth is never sent to a server, saved, or shared. Closing the page removes it. See the Privacy Policy for the full statement.

What if I was born on 29 February?

Your age still increases once a year. For both the age calculation and the next-birthday countdown, this calculator uses 28 February in years that are not leap years. The number of leap days you have lived through is shown separately in your result.

Can I calculate my age on a past or future date?

Yes — use the “Age on a date” tab. Enter your date of birth and any date from 1900 onwards, and it shows how old you were, or will be, on that date.

Why does my age differ from another calculator?

Most differences come from the reference date or time zone: a calculator using UTC while it is still yesterday (or already tomorrow) where you are can be a day out. Others divide total days by 365.25, which gives an approximation rather than a calendar age. This calculator uses your device’s local date and real calendar arithmetic.

Is this accurate enough for official documents?

For everyday use, yes — the arithmetic follows the standard calendar method. For passports, benefits, school admission or any legal purpose, always use the age stated on the official document or by the issuing authority, as their rules on cut-off dates can differ.

Does the calculator work for dates before 1900?

The date fields accept dates from 1 January 1900 onwards. That covers every living person and keeps the calendar rules (including the 1900 non-leap century year) correct.

Is this calculator free?

Yes. It is free to use, with no account, no sign-up and no limit on calculations.
